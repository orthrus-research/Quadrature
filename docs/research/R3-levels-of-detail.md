# R3: levels of detail

Requirement section 6. First pass, 2026-11-13. Basis marks are defined in
[README.md](README.md).

## 6.1 Representations

**Published approaches. [V]**
- **Voxel mip pyramid, meshed per level** (Kapoulkine, SIGGRAPH 2020): a sparse set of
  fixed-size chunks, each storing a mip pyramid; an octree of render leaves that split
  near the camera and merge far from it; leaves of 16 cubed voxels at 500 to 1000
  triangles each; seams hidden by skirt geometry with a depth bias.
- **Progressively ordered buffers for blocky voxels** (0fps, 2018): vertices snap to a
  coarser grid per level and morph between levels, so no seams or skirts are needed;
  neighboring chunks must agree on level along shared boundaries.
- **Sparse voxel directed acyclic graph** (Aokana, 2025): compact storage with a streaming
  system that avoids loading the scene into GPU memory at once; its level of detail still
  relies on mesh simplification, with reported quality problems in disconnected clusters.
- **Heightfield clipmaps**: resample a heightfield on a uniform grid. Not sufficient here:
  cubic worlds have overhangs, caves and floating terrain, and volumetric data is about an
  order of magnitude larger than a heightfield.

**Fit with what the projects already produce. [V]**
Riven's preview sampler emits each distant column as runs, without building cubes. Runs
are already a volumetric representation: several solid spans per column. A run-based
coarse level therefore needs no new generator output.

**Candidates to evaluate. [K]**
1. Voxel mips meshed with the near-terrain mesher. Keeps the blocky look and shares the
   shading path. Cost grows with surface area.
2. Column runs at reduced horizontal resolution, meshed directly. Compact where terrain is
   mostly solid; weak for dense cave systems.
3. A hybrid: runs for the far field, voxel mips for the middle distance.
A ray-marched structure is set aside for now because it would not share stages with near
terrain, which requirement 6 asks for.

**Storage. [M]**
No per-area figures were found for any representation. Scale for orientation only: a
32 km by 32 km world has about one billion one-meter columns, so full detail everywhere
is out of reach on any budget and each coarser level cuts the count by four.

## 6.2 Transitions. [V]

Three methods are documented: skirts with depth bias (cheap, generated for every chunk),
geomorphing through progressively ordered buffers (no seam geometry), and dithered
crossfade between levels [K]. Stitching algorithms are described in the literature as
complicated and are avoided by all three.

## 6.3 Streaming and refinement. [K, partly V]

Aokana's streaming design is the closest published reference. The requirement to show
coarse detail everywhere first and refine outward matches a top-down octree: the root
levels are built first and are always available as a fallback.

## 6.4 Lighting distant terrain. [K]

Distant terrain has no stored light. Sky exposure can come from the shadow stage or from
the terrain's own height data; block light either is dropped beyond a distance or is
carried as coarse emissive data per level. This is decided with the lighting design (R4).

## 6.5 What the server sends. [K, from the projects' own contracts]

Riven's terrain is a pure function of the seed and the world's recorded definitions. A
client that holds both can compute ungenerated distant terrain itself, so the server sends
nothing for it. Only terrain that differs from generation (player changes, and anything
generated by rules the client cannot evaluate) needs to be sent, as coarse data.
This gives clients the seed and definitions, which decision D12 accepts.
